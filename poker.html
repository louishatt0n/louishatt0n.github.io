Is my poker win rate real?

Telling a winning player from a lucky one, using my own numbers.

74,146hands tracked
4.81big blinds won per 100 hands
$40 → +$367starting bankroll → profit

The record

Six-handed no-limit hold'em online, January to April 2026. I moved up from $0.01/$0.02 to $0.10/$0.20 blinds, played four tables at a time, and only played a stake while my bankroll held at least 20 buy-ins for it. Win rate is measured in big blinds won per 100 hands (bb/100), so it compares across stakes.

The money is real. The question is whether it shows an edge, or a break-even player who ran well.

Why it's hard to tell

A single hand can swing by a hundred big blinds; skill is a small drift underneath. Trackers measure the swing as a standard deviation in bb/100; six-handed players typically sit around 80–110, and mine is 91.94. Change it in the calculator to see how much the answer depends on it.

The standard error is how much a measured win rate would move if you replayed the same number of hands with different cards:

standard error = standard deviation ÷ √(hands ÷ 100) = 91.94 ÷ √741 ≈ 3.38 bb/100 95% range ≈ 4.81 ± 1.96 × 3.38 = −1.8 to +11.4 bb/100

The range includes zero, so 74,000 hands can't rule out break-even. That holds for any standard deviation above about 67, which covers almost every six-handed player.

Luck in all-in pots

Part of the noise can be measured directly. PokerTracker's all-in adjusted result replaces the outcome of every pot where a player was all-in before the last card with what that pot was worth on average. Mine is $335, against $367 actually won: about $33 of the profit was luck in all-in pots.

What I take from it

  • The result is real; the edge isn't proven. At this win rate and my variance, ruling out break-even at 95% confidence takes about 140,000 hands.
  • Judge decisions, not results. Over a few thousand hands, results mostly measure variance, so a session is better judged by the quality of its decisions than by the money won.
  • Bankroll rules exist to survive the noise. Even with a genuine 4.81 bb/100 edge, a 20 buy-in bankroll kept at one stake has about a 10% chance of ever going broke. Moving down a stake when the bankroll shrinks is what keeps that risk small.

It's the same question as my Bitcoin strategy test: a good-looking result over a short sample is weak evidence until the sample is big enough.
